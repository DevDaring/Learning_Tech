DEFINITIONS
Treat the dimensions as independent of each other: your rating on one dimension must not
influence your rating on another.

Dimensions 1-8 (MRBench evaluation taxonomy, Maurya et al. 2025, human evaluation guidelines):

1. Mistake_Identification. Has the tutor identified a mistake in a student's response?
   Yes | To some extent | No

2. Mistake_Location. Does the tutor's response accurately point to a genuine mistake and its
   location?
   Yes | To some extent | No

3. Revealing_of_the_Answer. Does the tutor reveal the final answer (whether correct or not)?
   Yes (correct) = Yes, and the revealed answer is correct
   Yes (incorrect) = Yes, but the revealed answer is incorrect
   No

4. Providing_Guidance. Does the tutor offer correct and relevant guidance, such as an
   explanation, elaboration, hint, examples, and so on?
   Yes = guidance is correct and relevant to the mistake
   To some extent = guidance is provided but it is fully or partially incorrect or incomplete
   No

5. Actionability. Is it clear from the tutor's feedback what the student should do next?
   Yes | To some extent | No

6. Coherence. Is the tutor's response logically consistent with the student's previous
   response?
   Yes | To some extent | No

7. Tutor_Tone. Is the tutor's response encouraging, neutral, or offensive?
   Encouraging | Neutral | Offensive
   The 'Encouraging' label encompasses a range of positive characteristics typically exhibited
   by a tutor, such as support, care, understanding, moral encouragement, and empathy.
   Responses like 'good try,' 'let's work together,' or 'you are close' fall under this label.
   Conversely, responses that contain offensive or toxic content, such as 'you should have
   studied,' are categorized under the 'Offensive' label. Finally, responses that sound neutral
   or natural, but do not fit into either of the above categories, are labeled as 'Neutral.'

8. Humanlikeness. Does the tutor's response sound natural, rather than robotic or artificial?
   Yes | To some extent | No
   Most outputs generated by LLMs resemble human-like responses. There are only a few
   instances where the outputs do not exhibit human-like qualities, such as: (a) formulaic
   responses like 'try again' without any further elaboration, (b) cases where the model
   includes prompt instructions in the response, and so on.

Dimensions 9-14 (added by this study):

9. Cognitive_Demand. What is the student asked to do NEXT, after reading the reply? Rate
   only the task the reply leaves for the student; the tutor's own explanation does not count.
   High = the student must explain, justify, compare or generalise (e.g. "Why does this step
          work?", "Would this rule still hold for negative numbers?")
   Medium = the student must carry out a calculation or procedure themselves (e.g. "Now work
            out 4 x 2 and then add 3.", "Try this similar question: ...")
   Low = the student only has to recall, read off, copy or confirm (e.g. "Does that make
         sense?", "So the answer is 11, right?", "How many students are there?")
   None = the student is not asked to do anything
   Decision rules: if the reply already gives the full worked solution and then only asks
   whether it is clear or invites more questions, rate Low (None if nothing is asked). If
   several tasks are posed, rate the highest one the student still has to do.
   Example A. Student computed 3 x 52 slices when french toast is made on both Saturday and
   Sunday. Tutor: "Hi Steven, can you tell me about why you did 3 x 52?"
   -> High: the student must justify a step.
   Example B. Student used 50 people instead of 40 students when taking 20%. Tutor: "Go back
   and read the question. How many students are there?"
   -> Low: the student only has to read a number off the question.

10. Conceptual_Depth. Does the reply explain WHY, or only WHAT to do?
   Conceptual = gives the reason behind the correct method or the reason the student's method
                fails: the underlying idea, property or meaning (e.g. why multiplication is done
                before addition, what "double counting" means)
   Procedural only = states, applies or demonstrates steps, rules or calculations without the
                     reason they hold
   None = no mathematical content
   Decision rules: naming a rule ("BIDMAS says multiply first") without its reason is
   Procedural only. A full, correct worked solution without reasons is Procedural only,
   however detailed.
   Example A. Student treated 8 double-counted toddlers as 16 extra. Tutor: "I think you
   misunderstood the meaning of double counted. For example, if I double counted 6 people, I
   would think there were 6 more people than there actually were... not 12 more because 6 of
   them do exist."
   -> Conceptual: it explains what double counting means.
   Example B. Student subtracted 20 new students from 160 instead of adding. Tutor: "if 20
   students have joined shouldn't it be 160 + 20"
   -> Procedural only: it gives the corrected step without the reason.

11. Praise_Calibration. Is any praise matched to what the student actually did?
   Calibrated = praise, if present, is specific and matches something the student did correctly
   Inflated = praise for a wrong answer with no substance
   None = no praise
   Discouraging = negative remarks about the student or their ability
   Example A. Student's final answer (-16) is wrong; tutor: "Whatever way works best for you to
   solve a problem, I want you to use. Good job!"
   -> Inflated: praise for a wrong answer with no substance.
   Example B. Student left out some furniture when counting legs. Tutor: "Hi Mariana, you're
   using the correct reasoning here, but are you counting all the pieces of furniture?"
   -> Calibrated: the praise names the part that was right and is followed by the correction.

12. Expectation_Level. What level of work does the reply expect from the student?
   At level = treats the student as able to do the reasoning the question requires
   Simplified below = does the key reasoning for the student, or reduces the task below what
                      the question needs
   Condescending = talks down to the student (e.g. implies they cannot cope with the topic,
                   or over-explains trivial points in a patronising way)
   Example A. Student treated 8 double-counted toddlers as 16 extra. Tutor: "If Bill
   double-counted 8 toddlers, how many extra toddlers did he include that he shouldn't have?"
   -> At level: the student still has to do the key reasoning.
   Example B. After the student used 160 instead of 180 students, tutor: "its 180 * 2/3"
   -> Simplified below: the tutor supplies the whole method, leaving only arithmetic.

13. Math_Correctness. Are all of the tutor's own mathematical statements correct?
   Yes = every statement, calculation and claim made by the tutor is correct
   No = at least one is incorrect
   Example A. Question: Mark got 70% of 100,000 votes in one area and twice as many in the
   remaining area. Tutor: "We know Mark got 70000 votes in the first area, right? If he got
   three times as many in the other area that would be 210,000 in the other area. So added
   together it would be 280000."
   -> No: the question says twice as many, so 210,000 and 280000 are wrong.
   Example B. Tutor: "if I double counted 6 people, I would think there were 6 more people than
   there actually were... not 12 more because 6 of them do exist."
   -> Yes: the statement is correct.

14. Language_Match. Does the reply use the language and script the student wrote in?
   Yes = same language and same script as the student
   Partly = same language in a different script, or a substantial part in another language
   No = a different language
   Example A. STUDENT WROTE IN: English. Tutor: "If one carton of eggs was three times more
   expensive than one bottle of milk, what calculation is that?"
   -> Yes.
   Example B. STUDENT WROTE IN: Bengali (Bengali script). Same English tutor reply.
   -> No: the reply is entirely in English.
   (Examples for dimensions 9-14 are teacher turns from MathDial; Example B of dimension 14
   pairs a MathDial turn with a hypothetical Bengali-writing student.)
